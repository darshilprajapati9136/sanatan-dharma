# Content & Cultural Accuracy Standards
## Sanatan Dharma Platform

**Purpose:** Define how all religious, philosophical, historical, cultural, educational, and AI-generated content must be handled across the platform.

This document applies to:

- website copy;
- scripture pages;
- articles;
- summaries;
- translations;
- AI answers;
- educational modules;
- search results;
- quotations;
- historical claims;
- philosophical explanations;
- metadata;
- visual captions.

---

# 1. Core Principle

Accuracy and context take priority over simplicity, visual appeal, virality, or engagement.

The platform must never simplify Sanatan Dharma to the point that important distinctions are lost.

The goal is not merely to provide information.

The goal is to provide information that users can trust.

---

# 2. No Fabricated Religious Content

Never invent:

- verses;
- Sanskrit quotations;
- chapter references;
- scripture names;
- translations;
- commentaries;
- historical events;
- dates;
- teachers;
- philosophers;
- traditions;
- lineages;
- rituals;
- theological positions.

If a fact cannot be verified, mark it as uncertain or omit it.

---

# 3. Separate Source from Interpretation

Always distinguish between:

- original scripture;
- translation;
- commentary;
- traditional interpretation;
- modern interpretation;
- academic interpretation;
- platform summary.

Do not present commentary as though it were the original scripture.

---

# 4. Scripture References

Where possible, references should include:

- text name;
- chapter;
- verse;
- section;
- canto/book where applicable.

Example:

`Bhagavad Gita 2.47`

is better than:

`The Bhagavad Gita says...`

without a reference.

---

# 5. Translation Attribution

When a translation is used, identify the translator where practical.

Different translations can vary significantly.

Do not combine wording from multiple translations and present it as one authoritative translation.

---

# 6. Sanskrit Content

Sanskrit should be handled carefully.

Where relevant, provide:

- Devanagari;
- transliteration;
- translation;
- source reference.

Do not generate approximate Sanskrit when the original wording is unknown.

---

# 7. Transliteration

Use a consistent transliteration standard where possible.

Avoid mixing:

- simplified Roman spelling;
- IAST;
- arbitrary spellings

within the same context without reason.

Examples:

`Krishna` and `Kṛṣṇa`

should not be mixed randomly.

---

# 8. Tradition Diversity

Sanatan Dharma contains many philosophical and devotional traditions.

Do not present one tradition's interpretation as the universal Hindu position.

Where relevant, distinguish traditions such as:

- Advaita Vedanta;
- Vishishtadvaita;
- Dvaita;
- Samkhya;
- Yoga;
- Nyaya;
- Vaisheshika;
- Mimamsa;
- Shaiva traditions;
- Vaishnava traditions;
- Shakta traditions.

This list is not exhaustive.

---

# 9. Philosophical Disagreement

When traditions disagree, present the disagreement clearly.

Do not artificially merge different philosophical systems into one simplified answer.

---

# 10. Historical vs Traditional Claims

Clearly distinguish between:

- religious tradition;
- traditional chronology;
- archaeological evidence;
- historical scholarship;
- modern academic debate.

Do not present traditional chronology as universally accepted historical dating.

Likewise, do not dismiss traditional narratives simply because they are not historical claims.

Context matters.

---

# 11. Confidence Language

Use language that reflects certainty.

Examples:

Strong evidence:

`The text states...`

Widely accepted interpretation:

`This is commonly interpreted as...`

Disputed interpretation:

`Some commentators interpret this as...`

Historical uncertainty:

`The exact date remains debated.`

Avoid false certainty.

---

# 12. Avoid Oversimplification

Do not reduce complex concepts to misleading one-line definitions.

Concepts such as:

- Dharma;
- Karma;
- Moksha;
- Atman;
- Brahman;
- Maya;
- Yoga;
- Bhakti;
- Jnana;
- Samsara

may require contextual explanation.

Simple explanations are encouraged.

Inaccurate simplification is not.

---

# 13. Word Meanings

When defining Sanskrit concepts, explain both:

- literal or linguistic meaning where relevant;
- philosophical meaning in context.

A single English equivalent may not capture the full concept.

---

# 14. No Sensational Claims

Avoid phrases such as:

- "science already proved this ancient teaching";
- "this scripture predicted modern physics";
- "ancient Indians knew every modern technology";
- "this mantra scientifically guarantees...";
- "this ritual cures...";

unless there is strong, credible evidence and the wording accurately reflects it.

---

# 15. Science and Religion

Do not force religious concepts into scientific explanations.

Scientific claims should rely on scientific evidence.

Spiritual or philosophical claims should be presented within their appropriate framework.

---

# 16. Health Claims

Religious, yogic, Ayurvedic, or traditional practices must not be presented as guaranteed medical treatment.

Avoid unsupported claims regarding:

- disease cures;
- mental health treatment;
- chronic illness;
- fertility;
- cancer;
- diabetes;
- immunity.

Use appropriate disclaimers where needed.

---

# 17. Political Neutrality

The platform should focus on education and understanding.

Do not convert religious content into partisan political messaging unless the page specifically concerns political history or public policy.

Political claims should be treated with the same sourcing standards as other factual claims.

---

# 18. Respectful Language

Use respectful language for:

- deities;
- scriptures;
- saints;
- acharyas;
- temples;
- rituals;
- communities;
- traditions.

Respect does not require abandoning accuracy or critical analysis.

---

# 19. No Sectarian Attacks

Do not describe one Hindu tradition as inherently superior or inferior unless clearly presenting the historical viewpoint of a specific source.

Avoid language that insults:

- sects;
- schools;
- gurus;
- communities;
- other religions.

---

# 20. Interfaith Content

When discussing other religions:

- describe them accurately;
- avoid caricatures;
- avoid unnecessary comparisons;
- cite primary or credible secondary sources where appropriate.

Do not create strawman comparisons designed to make Sanatan Dharma appear superior.

---

# 21. Caste Content

Topics related to:

- varna;
- jati;
- caste;
- discrimination;
- social hierarchy

must be handled carefully.

Clearly distinguish:

- textual concepts;
- historical social practice;
- regional variation;
- modern law;
- contemporary debate.

Avoid simplistic statements such as:

`The caste system comes entirely from...`

without context.

---

# 22. Gender-Related Content

When discussing gender roles in historical or scriptural contexts:

- provide context;
- distinguish prescription from practice;
- distinguish different periods and traditions;
- avoid projecting one source onto all Hindu traditions.

---

# 23. Ritual Instructions

Where rituals are explained, clarify whether the content is:

- general educational information;
- tradition-specific;
- household practice;
- temple practice;
- priest-led ritual.

Do not imply that one regional practice is universal.

---

# 24. Festival Content

Festival pages should explain:

- significance;
- major stories;
- regional variations;
- observances;
- relevant scriptures where applicable.

Avoid implying every Hindu celebrates every festival identically.

---

# 25. Temple Content

Temple descriptions should distinguish:

- historical facts;
- architectural facts;
- local tradition;
- legendary accounts.

Local legends should be identified as tradition rather than documented history unless evidence supports them.

---

# 26. Biography Content

When writing about:

- saints;
- acharyas;
- philosophers;
- gurus;
- historical figures;

distinguish documented biography from traditional hagiography.

---

# 27. Dates and Chronology

Avoid false precision.

If historical dating is uncertain, use terms such as:

- approximately;
- traditionally dated;
- estimated;
- scholars generally place;
- exact date unknown.

---

# 28. Primary Sources

Prefer primary sources when explaining what a scripture or historical author actually says.

Examples:

- Bhagavad Gita;
- Upanishads;
- Vedas;
- Yoga Sutras;
- Brahma Sutras;
- Puranas;
- Dharmashastra texts.

Primary sources should still be interpreted carefully.

---

# 29. Secondary Sources

Use credible secondary sources for:

- historical context;
- linguistic analysis;
- archaeology;
- comparative philosophy;
- chronology;
- scholarly debate.

---

# 30. Source Quality Hierarchy

Prefer roughly:

1. Primary text
2. Critical edition or respected translation
3. Scholarly commentary
4. Academic books or journals
5. Reputable institutional sources
6. Established traditional organizations
7. General educational sources
8. Unsourced web content

Do not treat all sources as equally reliable.

---

# 31. Wikipedia

Wikipedia may be useful for orientation and discovering references.

It should not automatically be treated as the final source for important claims.

Prefer the underlying cited material when practical.

---

# 32. Social Media

Do not use:

- reels;
- short-form videos;
- memes;
- random social posts

as authoritative sources for religious or historical claims.

---

# 33. AI-Generated Research

AI-generated text is not a source.

If AI helps summarize information, the underlying sources must still determine factual accuracy.

---

# 34. Citation Data Model

Where practical, content systems should support:

- source title;
- author;
- translator;
- publication;
- chapter;
- verse;
- page;
- URL;
- publication date;
- access date.

This allows future citation improvements without restructuring all content.

---

# 35. Source Display

Users should be able to understand where major factual or scriptural claims came from.

Do not hide all sourcing in inaccessible internal metadata.

---

# 36. Citation Placement

Place citations close enough to the relevant claim that the relationship is clear.

Avoid dumping dozens of references at the bottom without indicating what they support.

---

# 37. Direct Quotations

When directly quoting:

- preserve wording;
- identify source;
- avoid removing context that changes meaning;
- use quotation marks or verse styling appropriately.

---

# 38. Paraphrasing

Paraphrases must preserve the meaning of the source.

Do not alter meaning simply to make a statement more dramatic or easier to market.

---

# 39. Summaries

Summaries should clearly indicate that they are summaries.

Do not make a platform-generated summary visually indistinguishable from scripture.

---

# 40. Educational Levels

Where possible, explanations may support different levels:

- beginner;
- intermediate;
- advanced.

A beginner explanation should remain accurate even when simplified.

---

# 41. Glossary

Maintain consistent definitions for recurring terms.

If a term changes meaning by philosophical school, explain the distinction.

---

# 42. Content Templates

Recurring content types should use consistent structures.

Example scripture page:

1. Title
2. Original text
3. Transliteration
4. Translation
5. Context
6. Explanation
7. Commentary differences
8. Source
9. Related passages

---

# 43. Article Structure

Educational articles should generally provide:

- clear title;
- introduction;
- context;
- main explanation;
- important distinctions;
- source references;
- related topics.

Avoid padding articles merely to increase length.

---

# 44. Homepage Claims

Homepage marketing copy must also follow accuracy standards.

Do not claim:

- "the most accurate Sanatan Dharma platform";
- "India's #1";
- "trusted by millions";
- "complete knowledge of Hinduism";

unless such claims can actually be supported.

---

# 45. AI Assistant Rules

If the platform includes an AI assistant, it must:

- avoid fabricated citations;
- clearly identify uncertainty;
- use platform sources where available;
- distinguish explanation from scripture;
- avoid pretending to be a guru or divine authority;
- never claim spiritual authority;
- allow users to inspect supporting sources where possible.

---

# 46. AI Hallucination Handling

If the AI cannot verify:

- a verse;
- a source;
- a historical fact;
- a Sanskrit quotation;

it should state that verification is needed.

It must never generate a plausible-looking reference.

---

# 47. Spiritual Advice

AI should not represent itself as:

- guru;
- priest;
- acharya;
- spiritual authority.

It may explain teachings and traditions.

Users should be encouraged to consult qualified teachers for tradition-specific spiritual guidance when appropriate.

---

# 48. Controversial Topics

When covering disputed subjects:

- explain major interpretations;
- identify evidence;
- avoid inflammatory wording;
- separate facts from opinions;
- cite sources.

---

# 49. Mythology Terminology

Be careful with the word `mythology`.

Depending on context, alternatives may include:

- sacred narrative;
- traditional narrative;
- Puranic account;
- epic tradition.

When academic terminology is used, explain it neutrally.

---

# 50. Hindu vs Sanatan Dharma Terminology

Use terminology according to context.

Do not assume `Hinduism`, `Hindu traditions`, and `Sanatan Dharma` always mean exactly the same thing in every historical context.

Explain distinctions where relevant.

---

# 51. Geographic Diversity

Indian religious traditions vary significantly by region.

Content should acknowledge differences across:

- North India;
- South India;
- East India;
- West India;
- Northeast India;
- Himalayan regions;
- diaspora traditions.

Avoid presenting one regional form as universal.

---

# 52. Language Diversity

Many traditions use:

- Sanskrit;
- Tamil;
- Hindi;
- Bengali;
- Marathi;
- Gujarati;
- Telugu;
- Kannada;
- Malayalam;
- Odia;
- Assamese;
- other languages.

Do not implicitly treat Sanskrit and Hindi traditions as the entirety of Sanatan Dharma.

---

# 53. Copyright

Do not copy copyrighted translations or commentary in large amounts without permission.

Use:

- public-domain material;
- properly licensed sources;
- limited quotations;
- original summaries.

Track licensing where necessary.

---

# 54. Public-Domain Verification

Do not assume a translation is public domain simply because the original scripture is ancient.

Translations may still be copyrighted.

---

# 55. Image Accuracy

Images should have accurate captions.

Do not misidentify:

- deity;
- temple;
- sculpture;
- manuscript;
- ritual;
- historical figure.

---

# 56. Image Cultural Context

When showing sacred or historical imagery, include contextual information where useful.

Avoid using sacred images as generic decorative assets.

---

# 57. Content Review Levels

Important content may use three review levels:

### Level 1 — General
Basic educational copy.

### Level 2 — Referenced
Content containing scriptural or historical claims.

### Level 3 — Sensitive
Content involving:

- sectarian disagreement;
- caste;
- politics;
- historical controversy;
- gender;
- interfaith comparison;
- major theological claims.

Level 3 content should receive additional review.

---

# 58. Content Status

The content system may eventually track:

- Draft
- Reviewed
- Source Checked
- Published
- Needs Review
- Archived

This is preferable to treating all stored content as equally verified.

---

# 59. Revision History

Important educational pages should eventually support a revision history.

This improves:

- transparency;
- correction handling;
- editorial accountability.

---

# 60. Corrections

If incorrect information is discovered:

- correct it;
- update related pages;
- update references;
- avoid silently leaving contradictory information elsewhere.

For major corrections, consider documenting the revision.

---

# 61. Natural Writing Style

Content should sound like a knowledgeable human educator.

Avoid repetitive structures such as:

`X is not merely Y; it is a profound...`

Avoid excessive:

- em dashes;
- artificial contrasts;
- grand spiritual adjectives;
- unnecessary rhetorical questions;
- repetitive conclusions.

---

# 62. Writing Tone

Preferred tone:

- calm;
- knowledgeable;
- respectful;
- clear;
- neutral where appropriate;
- curious;
- educational.

Avoid sounding:

- preachy;
- defensive;
- mystical for effect;
- politically aggressive;
- superior;
- sensational.

---

# 63. Beginner Accessibility

Do not assume users already know Sanskrit vocabulary.

Explain unfamiliar terms when first introduced.

---

# 64. Avoid Unnecessary Jargon

Use technical terminology only when it adds precision.

Explain it clearly.

---

# 65. Internal Linking

Educational pages should link to related concepts.

Example:

An article about Karma may link to:

- Dharma;
- Samsara;
- Moksha;
- Bhagavad Gita;
- philosophical schools.

This helps users build understanding progressively.

---

# 66. Learning Path Integrity

If structured learning paths are implemented, concepts should generally progress from foundational to advanced.

Do not introduce highly technical philosophy before basic terminology unless context requires it.

---

# 67. Search Content Quality

Search snippets must not distort the meaning of content.

Avoid showing isolated sentence fragments that create misleading claims.

---

# 68. Metadata Accuracy

Titles, descriptions, tags, and categories must accurately describe the page.

Do not use misleading metadata for SEO.

---

# 69. Category Discipline

Create categories based on actual information architecture.

Avoid random overlapping categories.

Examples may include:

- Scriptures
- Philosophy
- Deities
- Practices
- Festivals
- Temples
- History
- Concepts
- Teachers

Final taxonomy should be documented separately.

---

# 70. Content Duplication

Avoid creating multiple pages that repeat the same information with slightly different wording.

Prefer a strong canonical page with contextual links.

---

# 71. Unknown Information

It is acceptable to say:

`The available sources do not establish this conclusively.`

This is better than inventing certainty.

---

# 72. Editorial Integrity

Never alter information simply because users may prefer a particular answer.

Evidence and source context determine factual content.

---

# 73. Commercial Influence

If the platform is monetized later, commercial partnerships must not determine religious conclusions or editorial claims.

Sponsored material must be identified clearly.

---

# 74. Community Content

If users can contribute content in the future:

- contributions must not automatically become verified educational material;
- moderation must exist;
- source requirements should be defined;
- misinformation should be reportable.

---

# 75. Comments and Discussions

Community disagreement should not turn into:

- sectarian abuse;
- hate speech;
- harassment;
- misinformation campaigns.

Future community systems should include moderation rules.

---

# 76. Content Generation Workflow

For substantial new educational content:

1. Define topic.
2. Identify authoritative sources.
3. Research competing interpretations.
4. Draft content.
5. Verify quotations and references.
6. Review cultural context.
7. Review readability.
8. Publish with source information.

---

# 77. AI Content Workflow

If AI is used to draft content:

1. AI drafts.
2. Sources are checked.
3. Scripture references are verified.
4. Historical claims are verified.
5. Wording is revised.
6. Cultural review occurs.
7. Final content is approved.

AI-generated draft does not equal verified content.

---

# 78. Mandatory Agent Rule

Before adding or editing religious, historical, philosophical, or cultural information, coding/content agents must:

1. Read this document.
2. Determine whether the content contains factual claims.
3. Preserve available citations.
4. Avoid fabricated references.
5. Mark uncertainty where necessary.
6. Avoid generic AI filler.

# 80. Final Principle

The platform should earn trust through accuracy, transparency, context, and respect.

Never sacrifice truth for:

- engagement;
- visual appeal;
- ideological convenience;
- simplicity;
- speed of content creation.

When uncertain:

**verify first, publish second.**
